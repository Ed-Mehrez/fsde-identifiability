# Audit of the Gate 1 pilot (2026-11-11)

This is a second pass over stage A (fBm), stage B (real 2026 data) and the H-rescaling test, looking for errors that could change the conclusions. The scripts are `pilot/audit_gate1.py` and `pilot/audit_horizons.py`.

**Net effect.** The core stage-A finding stands and is robust to the choice of horizon window. Three claims were overstated and are corrected below: the label-free diagnostic's strength, the real-data p-values, and the "long memory" reading of the reservoirs. Nothing was found that reverses a conclusion.

## Checked and fine

- **No training contamination.** On Hugging Face, the weights were last committed on 2025-10-30 (Chronos-2), 2025-10-01 (TimesFM-2.5) and 2024-11-13 (Chronos-Bolt). The 2026 commits to the Chronos-2 repository are README edits. Every stage-B target is from 2026.
- **TimesFM output layout.** Column 0 is the mean, columns 1–9 are q0.1–q0.9, and column 5 is the median. This matches the `[:, :, 1:10]` slice used.
- **fBm oracle.** It conditions on B_1..B_L with B_0 = 0 known, which is one increment more information than the models have, 512 steps back. That is negligible. Its own coverage was 0.77–0.82 and its implied H was within 0.005 of the truth.
- **Reservoir data are not interpolated.** The contexts contain essentially no exactly-zero first or second differences.

## Errors and overstatements

1. **The stage A label-free diagnostic was overstated.** The headline ρ = −0.82 was computed across model × H cells, where the context Ĥ is almost exactly the true H. It mostly restates "coverage falls as H rises". Within a fixed H, the per-series Spearman ρ between the gap and long-horizon coverage averages **−0.17** (range −0.40 to −0.01 over 27 cells). The diagnostic carries some information beyond H, but it is weak.
2. **Stage B p-values are overstated.** Origins are weekly and horizons are 64 days, so consecutive target windows share 57 days, and 2026 holds only 3–4 non-overlapping target periods per domain. With Newey–West (lag 9) correction:
   - Crypto price, electricity (Chronos-2, Bolt) and reservoirs (Chronos-2, TimesFM) remain at p < 0.02.
   - Crypto RV is not significant for Chronos-2 or Bolt.
   - Reservoirs with Bolt drops to p = 0.08.
   
   Even so, with only 3–4 independent periods, the honest evidence is the *sign consistency* of the per-period ρ, not the p-values. For example, crypto price with Bolt gives −0.11, −0.25, −0.27, −0.22, and electricity with Chronos-2 gives −0.37, −0.40, +0.07, −0.19.
3. **The "persistent" reservoirs are trend-dominated, not fractional long memory.** In the context-H > 0.7 bins, 71–73% of windows have second-difference roughness above 0.95 (median 0.999), so the paths are locally smooth. A variogram exponent near 1 is the signature of a trend (seasonal drawdown or filling), which our H estimators cannot tell apart from long memory because they cap at 1. The reservoir result should be read as "models under-cover on trending series", not "on long-memory series".
4. **Common shocks dominate real-data coverage levels.** For example, every method, including the baseline, covers only 0.69–0.77 on crypto prices in 2026. Absolute coverage numbers on real data describe a few 2026 market and weather paths, not stable properties of the models. Comparisons between methods at the same windows are more reliable than absolute levels.

## Minor

- **TimesFM non-negativity.** `infer_is_positive` is on by default, so for electricity zones whose context is all positive, TimesFM's quantiles are floored at 0. This slightly affects its electricity lower tail.
- **Scale weighting.** "Loss ÷ baseline" is a ratio of sums, so it weights high-scale series more. This matters most for electricity in EUR/MWh.
- **Survivorship.** Crypto selection keeps pairs listed through 2026-06, which is mild survivorship.

## On horizons: should they depend on H, and is that cheating?

All results use the same horizon blocks for every H, model and baseline. That is the conservative choice. The full profile shows the conclusions do not depend on the 33–64 window:

| 80% coverage by horizon block | 1–8 | 9–16 | 17–32 | 33–64 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| fBm H = 0.8: oracle | 0.82 | 0.83 | 0.81 | 0.79 |
| fBm H = 0.8: Chronos-2 / TimesFM-2.5 | 0.80 / 0.81 | 0.75 / 0.76 | 0.70 / 0.73 | 0.64 / 0.65 |
| Reservoirs, context H > 0.85: baseline | 0.79 | 0.79 | 0.79 | 0.81 |
| Reservoirs, context H > 0.85: Bolt / TimesFM-2.5 | 0.87 / 0.88 | 0.78 / 0.77 | 0.73 / 0.75 | 0.69 / 0.70 |
| Reservoirs, context H < 0.3: Bolt / TimesFM-2.5 | 0.82 / 0.80 | 0.79 / 0.80 | 0.80 / 0.80 | 0.79 / 0.78 |

The effect grows steadily with horizon where H is high and is absent where H is low.

**When H-dependent horizons would be cheating:**

- choosing them after looking at outcomes;
- using the true H or future data to choose them;
- using them in the cross-series H-versus-failure analysis. Every method gets worse at longer horizons, so giving high-H series longer horizons would manufacture an H–failure correlation.

**When they would be legitimate:**

- the rule is fixed in advance, computed from the context only, applied to all methods, scored relative to a reference at the same horizon, and reported alongside the full profile.

A principled version exists for mean-reverting series: evaluate within the range of lags where the context variogram is still growing. Beyond the decorrelation time, the forecast tends to the stationary distribution and H no longer matters. For fBm there is no natural horizon: self-similarity means the mismatch grows like k^(H − H_fc) at every scale.

## Revised conclusions

- **Stage A (fBm): stands.** The models compress H and lose long-horizon coverage at H ≥ 0.7, increasingly with horizon. The label-free gap is a weak per-series predictor (within-H ρ ≈ −0.17), not a strong one.
- **Stage B (real data): inconclusive rather than "weak pass".** Directionally consistent on prices, electricity and trending reservoirs, but with 3–4 independent periods per domain and trend confounding. More independent target periods are needed, from more domains or later data.
- **H-rescaling: the negative result stands.** On real data the context variogram is the wrong reference for how conditional forecast uncertainty should grow.
